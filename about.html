I’m studying Computer Engineering at Northeastern, and I spend most of my time on robotics — specifically on making simulation fast and differentiable enough that muscle-driven models can actually learn to move. What pulled me in was sim2real: the gap between a policy that works in a simulator and one that works on hardware, and how much of that gap is really a physics problem.

Right now that means research at NEUMove on compliant muscle-tendon units, leading a student group building the ORCA Hand, and contributing to MuJoCo and MuJoCo Warp upstream. There’s a fuller account on the research page.

Outside of that

I hike, and occasionally climb something with a summit on it. Carrauntoohil in Ireland started it; Fuji was the one I didn’t finish. The Ak-Suu traverse in Kyrgyzstan has been an obsession lately, though it stays theoretical for now. There’s a log of the ones I’ve done.

I’ve also come back around to reading. I disliked it thoroughly from about middle school through high school, and it turns out that was mostly about being assigned things. Classics, psychology, and fantasy, mainly. Currently working through The Brothers Karamazov, slowly. Recommendations very welcome.
